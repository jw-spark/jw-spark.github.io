Day 2｜26 Oct (Mon) Taipei

① Today's Theme: Taipei Zoo family day + Linjiang St. Tourist Night Market (Tonghua Night Market) for local food in the evening

② Detailed Timeline

TimeItinerary
9:00 AMTake the MRT Wenhu Line to Taipei Zoo Station, ~1-minute walk from Exit 1 into the park
9:00 AM-12:30 PMTaipei Zoo — Giant Panda House, Penguin House, Koala House (indoor, air-conditioned, weather-proof); make use of the free park shuttle train (NT$5/ride) to reach the Bird Park area. Seniors 65+ enter free, children under 6 enter free
NoonLunch at the zoo's food court (wide selection, including kids' meals)
1:30-4:00 PMContinue leisurely touring the zoo, or leave early to rest at the hotel — flexible depending on the seniors' energy levels
4:30 PMTake the Wenhu Line, transfer to the Tamsui-Xinyi Line (red line) to Xinyi Anhe Station, ~25-30 min total
5:30-7:30 PMLinjiang St. Tourist Night Market (Tonghua Night Market) — ~5-minute walk from Exit 3; recommend Liang Ji Braised Foods, Yupinyuan Ice & Fire Tang Yuan, and Legendary Sweet Potato Balls (Michelin Bib Gourmand picks)
From 7:30 PMTake the red line back to the Taipei Main Station hotel, early to bed

③ Transport Guide

  • Taipei Main Station → Zoo Station: direct on the MRT Wenhu Line (brown line), ~30-35 min, no transfer needed
  • Zoo Station → Xinyi Anhe Station: transfer from Wenhu Line to Tamsui-Xinyi Line (red line), ~25-30 min
  • Xinyi Anhe Station → Linjiang St. Tourist Night Market: ~5-minute walk from Exit 3
  • Linjiang St. Tourist Night Market → Taipei Main Station: direct red line, ~15-20 min

④ Family & Senior-Friendly Tips

Helpful Tips
  • The zoo is quite large (165 hectares) — make use of the free park shuttle train to "ride up, then stroll back down," so seniors don't need to climb any hills.
  • Seniors 65+ enter free with ID, children under 6 enter free — remember to bring identification for verification.
  • The Giant Panda House, Penguin House, and Koala House are all indoor, air-conditioned exhibits — plan for more time indoors if it's hot or raining that day.
  • Stalls at Linjiang St. Tourist Night Market are concentrated along a roughly 300-meter main strip, unlike Shilin Night Market which requires much more walking — great for seniors. Choose milder options like pan-fried buns and braised foods, avoiding overly spicy stalls.
  • The night market stays open late, but the main stalls are busiest from 18:00-21:00 — recommend arriving before 19:00 and leaving around 20:30 to avoid returning to the hotel too late.

⑤ Rainy Day Alternative

Rain Contingency
  • The zoo's Giant Panda House, Penguin House, and Koala House are all indoor exhibits — the whole visit can be shifted indoors on a rainy day with minimal impact.
  • Linjiang St. Tourist Night Market stalls are mostly semi-covered, so light rain has little effect; bring an umbrella. If it rains heavily, consider dining at a nearby department store in the Xinyi shopping district instead.

① Today's Theme: Train south to Jiaoxi, the most relaxed photo-op attraction in the afternoon, and first hot spring experience in the evening

② Detailed Timeline

TimeItinerary
9-10 AMCheck out of the Taipei hotel, head to Taipei Main Station
10 AM-NoonTake the TRA train to Jiaoxi (~60-80 min journey)
NoonArrive at Jiaoxi Train Station, take Hotel PIN Jiaoxi's free shuttle (or ~5-minute taxi) to check in; lunch in the Jiaoxi area (recommend Sanxing Scallion Pancakes, Yilan Cake, Pork with Sweet Potato Starch and other local snacks, avoiding beef dishes)
2-4 PMJimmy SPA Park — ~15-20 minute taxi ride from Jiaoxi, on the south side of Yilan Train Station; installation art for photos, flat terrain, no climbing needed
EveningTaxi back to Jiaoxi (~15-20 min); eat around the Jiaoxi area (many hot spring eateries and souvenir shops within walking distance), then soak in the hotel's hot spring to relax, early to bed

③ Transport Guide

  • Taipei Main Station → Jiaoxi Station: TRA Tze-Chiang Express/Puyuma Express/Taroko Express ~60-80 min, fare ~NT$277 (regular local trains are ~NT$179 but take longer — reserved-seat express trains are recommended for comfort)
  • There's no HSR to Jiaoxi — only the TRA train or the Kamalan Bus (boarding at Banqiao Station/Taipei Bus Station/City Hall Bus Station, ~80-110 min journey, fare NT$140-150)
  • Jiaoxi Station → Hotel PIN Jiaoxi: free hotel shuttle (book a day ahead by phone), or ~5-minute taxi ride
  • Jiaoxi → Yilan City (Jimmy SPA Park, etc.): ~15-20 minute taxi, fare ~NT$250-300
  • Book TRA tickets in advance via the website/app (up to 28 days ahead) to secure reserved seats, especially for weekday travel

④ Family & Senior-Friendly Tips

Helpful Tips
  • Reserved-seat TRA trains have luggage storage areas — book online in advance and choose aisle seats to make it easier for seniors to move around.
  • Jimmy SPA Park is free and requires little standing — a great way to ease into Yilan. If the seniors are tired, they can also stay at the Jiaoxi hotel to soak in the hot spring and head out for food later in the evening.
  • Every room at Hotel PIN Jiaoxi comes with its own hot spring tub, so guests can soak right away to ease the fatigue of the journey; the lobby also offers complimentary welcome snacks from the afternoon onward — a nice place to rest too.

⑤ Rainy Day Alternative

Rain Contingency
  • Some installations at Jimmy SPA Park have covered walkways, so light rain has little impact. If it rains heavily, consider the Yilan Distillery (indoor, tastings and tours available) or the Formosa Chuanqi Sugar Refinery (indoor DIY experience), or simply stay at the hotel for hot springs and the play room, both unaffected by weather.

  • NCFTA has plenty of rest areas and handrails in the restrooms — widely regarded as one of Yilan's most senior-friendly attractions.

⑤ Rainy Day Alternative

Rain Contingency

  • If it rains heavily, switch the afternoon plan to iFun Anyoung World (an indoor family attraction with AR interactive exhibits, entirely indoors).

Day 5｜29 Oct (Thu) Yilan

① Today's Theme: Meihua Lake + Luodong Night Market — Yilan's most senior-friendly lakeside stroll, followed by local food in the evening

② Detailed Timeline

TimeItinerary
9:30-11:30 AMMeihua Lake Scenic Area — a ~4km lakeside path, flat with pavilions and benches along the way; stroll, cycle, or rent an electric boat to tour the lake. Widely regarded as one of the most senior-friendly attractions in Yilan
NoonLunch near Meihua Lake or back in the city
2-4 PMA relaxed activity — Longtan Lake Park (also flat and free of charge) or head straight back to the hotel for a nap, flexible depending on the seniors' energy levels
5-6 PMLuodong Night Market — arrive early to avoid the 18:30-20:30 crowd peak; recommend Dragon Phoenix Legs, Bao Xin Fen Yuan (tapioca balls), and Pork with Sweet Potato Starch, avoiding stalls with long queues

③ Transport Guide

  • Jiaoxi → Meihua Lake: recommend calling a taxi directly, ~25-30 min, fare ~NT$400-450
  • Meihua Lake/Longtan Lake → Luodong Night Market: ~15-20 minute taxi; Luodong Night Market → Jiaoxi: ~20-25 min journey — check taxi-hailing options ahead of time or ask the hotel to help arrange one

④ Family & Senior-Friendly Tips

Helpful Tips
  • Meihua Lake has little shade in summer/autumn — bring hats for sun protection and a folding chair for seniors to rest whenever needed.
  • Luodong Night Market can get crowded — go at an off-peak time and eat in a "spread out" style (everyone picks a different stall to reduce queueing).

⑤ Rainy Day Alternative

Rain Contingency
  • Most of the Meihua Lake path is uncovered — if it rains heavily, switch to the indoor Yilan Art Museum or the Zhongxing Cultural and Creative Park (indoor exhibitions + markets).
  • Luodong Night Market stalls are mostly semi-covered, so light rain has little impact; bring an umbrella.

④ Family & Senior-Friendly Tips

Helpful Tips
  • Today is deliberately kept as a "transition and recovery" day, without too much scheduled, letting everyone recover from the string of outings in Yilan.
  • If seniors or the little one are tired, they can stay at the hotel to rest in the afternoon and head out to Sanchong in the evening instead.
  • Ginger duck hot pot broth is simmered with sesame oil and ginger — warming in nature, so seniors should eat in moderation and not overeat; children can opt for lighter sides (like cabbage or noodles) to avoid an upset stomach at night.
  • The Bawei Ginger Duck Hot Pot flagship store doesn't take reservations and only operates in autumn/winter (roughly October to the following spring) — check their Facebook page beforehand to confirm they're open that day.

⑤ Rainy Day Alternative

Rain Contingency
  • Taipei Main Station itself is a large indoor mall (underground mall + station hall) — shopping and dining within the station area is completely unaffected by rain.
  • Sanchong Bawei Ginger Duck Hot Pot is an indoor dining venue (three floors) — completely unaffected by rain, no itinerary adjustment needed.

4. Packing & Practical Tips

✅ Documents & Finances

Essential Documents

  • Passport (valid for 6+ months) with photo backup saved on phone
  • Digital copies of flight tickets/boarding passes
  • Hotel booking confirmations (both Chinese and English, to help taxi drivers verify addresses)
  • Travel insurance policy

Finance & Communication

  • NT dollar cash (some food stalls and markets don't accept credit cards)
  • EasyCard (available at the airport, usable on MRT/buses/some shops)
  • Credit card (usable at Nanmen Market, Carrefour, and chain restaurants)
  • International SIM card or WiFi hotspot device (purchase beforehand at Changi Airport or online)

✅ For Seniors & Children

Senior Essentials

  • Regular medications (blood pressure, digestive, etc. — recommend carrying in carry-on, not checked)
  • Comfortable, non-slip walking shoes (this itinerary involves a lot of walking — shoes matter more than luggage)
  • A warm jacket (for the early arrival and after hot spring soaks — October mornings/evenings in Taiwan can be cool)
  • Reading glasses/spare hearing aid batteries

Children's Essentials

  • Regular children's medication (fever reducer, digestive medicine, mosquito repellent)
  • Lightweight stroller (useful for the longer walks at Meihua Lake, NCFTA, the zoo, etc.)
  • Hot spring bath toys for kids (Hotel PIN Jiaoxi rooms already have private hot spring tubs)
  • A comfort toy/familiar item (useful during the red-eye flight and in unfamiliar surroundings)

✅ Clothing & Other Items

Clothing

  • Light raincoat/folding umbrella (rain is possible in both Taipei and Yilan in October)
  • Non-slip, waterproof shoes (needed for zoo paths and lakeside walkways)
  • Swimwear for the hot springs (required for the outdoor SPA area at Hotel PIN Jiaoxi)
  • One warm jacket (for morning/evening temperature swings and air-conditioned rooms)

Other Items

  • Charger/power bank, plug adapter (Taiwan uses 110V, different sockets from Singapore)
  • A small reusable bag (for souvenir and market shopping)
  • Wet wipes and hand sanitizer (before/after eating at night markets and markets)
  • A reusable water bottle (most attractions provide water fountains for refills)

✅ Insurance & Other Notes

Insurance & Other Notes
  • Recommend purchasing travel insurance that covers flight delays/cancellations, especially given the seniors traveling along and the possibility of a lingering typhoon in late October.
  • The return flight TR875 departs at 00:15 (a red-eye flight) — the family should avoid packing 31 Oct too tightly, to conserve energy.
  • For TRA reserved seats between Jiaoxi and Taipei, book in advance via the website/app (up to 28 days ahead) — weekend trains are popular, so lock them in early.
  • All meals throughout the trip avoid beef-based dishes (e.g., beef noodles, mutton soup are optional if desired), focusing on local Yilan/Taipei pork, seafood, and noodle dishes instead.
  • Sanchong Bawei Ginger Duck Hot Pot only operates in autumn/winter — check their Facebook page beforehand to confirm they're open that day.
  • Seniors 65+ enter the zoo free, children under 6 enter free — bring identification for verification.

5. Core Itinerary Principles Recap

📋 Core Itinerary Principles Recap

  • Balanced pacing: One major attraction each morning, 1-2 relaxed activities each afternoon, ensuring seniors/child have plenty of rest time.
  • Simple transport: Rely entirely on TRA trains + MRT + taxis for short distances — no self-driving, no complicated transit passes.
  • Concentrated accommodation: Taipei Main Station area – Roaders Plus Hotel (3 nights) + Jiaoxi – Hotel PIN Jiaoxi (3 nights), only one hotel change for the entire trip.
  • Pitfalls avoided: Skips long-queue trendy spots, steep pure-hiking routes, cultural/creative parks/Taipei 101/Jiufen/Shifen/National Palace Museum/Yuanshan, and the Maokong Gondola (closed for maintenance the Monday of the week of 26 Oct, not a trial-run day).
  • All requested attractions included: Taipei Rear Station, Q Square, Underground Mall, Taipei Zoo, Linjiang St. Tourist Night Market, Sanchong Bawei Ginger Duck Hot Pot, Nanmen Market, and Ningxia Night Market are all incorporated, with Day 1, Day 2, and Day 7 having zero overlap.
  • Red-eye-flight friendly: The final day stays entirely within walking distance of Taipei Main Station, with an afternoon nap reserved and a direct trip on the Airport MRT right across from the hotel in the evening — no need to head back into the city.
